Welcome to the Meadow Lab

About 10–15 minutes · ages 8–11

Dr. Sage

Hello! I study meadows. In this notebook you will build a habitat, test a prediction about water and sunlight, and fix a meadow that has lost its balance.

No login, no score, no timer. Nothing here is a race.

What you will be able to do

  • Tell a from a in a meadow.
  • Explain how sunlight and water reach plants, and how plants reach animals.
  • Trace a simple food chain and say which way the moves.
  • Predict what happens when one condition changes — then check it in the model.
  • Repair a struggling habitat, and say why your repair worked.

Before you start: this is a model, not a real meadow

A is a simplified copy. Real meadows have hundreds of species, weather, soil and seasons. Our meadow has a handful of species and two dials. Real ecologists make models like this too, then test them outdoors. Every number you see is a model value — an illustrative number, not a scientific measurement.

Species you can place

Use the Add and Remove buttons (no dragging needed). Add removes nothing else: your meadow keeps what you put there.

Hint: what does “viable” mean here?

A habitat is viable in this model when every animal you placed has at least one food that is also in the meadow. If an animal has no food at all, it will lose energy every step and its numbers will fall.

Step 4 · Run the test

Lower the slider for water supply in the Conditions you control panel, then move the model forward. Use Step forward to watch one step at a time, or Run 6 steps to see the whole change.

Hint: how do I make a fair test?

Change one condition and leave the other alone. If you change water and sunlight together, you cannot tell which one caused what you see. That is why the notebook asks you to choose one.

Activity 3 · Restore balance

Diagnose, repair, retry as often as you like

Something has gone wrong in a meadow. Read the indicators, find the cause, and set the conditions so the meadow recovers.

Choose a rescue job

Rescue goal

    Steps in a row that meet the goal:

    Replace a species that has disappeared

    In this model a species that reaches zero does not return on its own. If you want it back, put it back — a real conservation project would do the same. Adding a species counts as an organism choice, not as a change of conditions.

    Balance restored

    Hint: where should I look first?

    Look at the two resource bars. If soil water is low, plants cannot grow no matter how much light they get. If sunlight is too high, the soil dries out faster and shade plants struggle. Then check which animal has lost its food.

    Free explore

    No rules, no goal

    Now you can play with the meadow. Change anything, at any time, and watch what happens step by step.

    • Try full sun with almost no water.
    • Try deep shade with plenty of water — which plant still grows?
    • Take away every plant-eater and see what the predator does.

    Reminder

    The meadow never shows animals being caught. The model counts how many animals lose energy and how many survive to the next step. Real hunting is not shown, and real meadows are far more complicated than this one.

    Your lesson summary

    Next practice

    Take it outside. Find a patch of grass, or a plant pot. Write down three living things you can see in five minutes and guess which is a producer and which is a consumer. Look for one food link you can actually observe — a chewed leaf counts.

    Then ask: what would change here if it stopped raining for two weeks? You have already tested that question in the model.
